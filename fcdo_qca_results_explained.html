Part 1: The Question

The Traditional Question

“Which factors predict success?”

Regression logic: each factor in isolation

Problem: Assumes factors work independently — but in reality, success depends on combinations

The Configurational Question

“What combinations of factors lead to success?”

QCA logic: configurations, not individual effects

Key insight: Multiple pathways can lead to the same outcome (equifinality)

  • Traditional evaluation asks “What predicts success?” — each factor in isolation.
  • We ask “What combinations of factors reliably lead to success?” — that’s configurationalism.

Part 3: The Method

What is QCA?

Qualitative Comparative Analysis asks:

“What combinations of conditions are associated with an outcome?”

Traditional regression

  • Effect of X on Y, controlling for Z
  • Assumes variables work independently
  • “On average, funding flexibility increases impact by 0.3 points”

QCA approach

  • What combinations lead to success?
  • Conditions work together
  • “Funding flexibility + local embedding + GESI focus → equity outcomes”

The Key Question

Which conditions matter for success?

NECESSARY
"Can't succeed without it"
SUFFICIENT
"If present, always succeeds"
QCA FINDS
Both types

QCA finds both: necessary (must-haves) AND sufficient (guarantees success)

Two Types of Relationships

Necessary Conditions

Must be present for the outcome to occur

Example

“You need a passport to travel internationally”

No passport → Cannot travel Having passport → Doesn’t guarantee travel

Test: Of all successful cases, how many have this condition?

Sufficient Conditions

Guarantee the outcome when present

Example

“A winning lottery ticket guarantees money”

Winning ticket → Definitely get money No ticket → Could still get money other ways

Test: Of all cases with this condition, how many succeed?

The Key Insight: Prevalence Shapes Our Analysis

When outcomes are RARE (16%)

Necessity analysis is informative

  • If 100% of successes have a feature, that’s meaningful
  • Finding “recipes” is hard (few successful cases)

Example: Exceptional Performance

When outcomes are COMMON (80%)

Sufficiency analysis is informative

  • Many conditions will seem “necessary” by chance
  • Finding reliable “recipes” is easier

Example: Impact Achievement

Why this matters

We use different analytical strategies for different outcomes based on how common they are.

See: Schneider & Wagemann (2012) on “trivialness”; Braumoeller & Goertz (2000) on necessary conditions methodology

Understanding QCA Metrics

🎯 Necessity Consistency
Question:** When outcome is present, is the condition also present?
Threshold:** ≥0.90 (condition in 90%+ of successful cases)
High = condition is a "must-have"
📏 Sufficiency Consistency (incl.cut)
Question:** When condition pattern is present, does outcome follow?
Our threshold:** incl.cut = 0.85 (85% of cases succeed)
High = pathway reliably produces success
📊 Coverage
Question:** What % of successful cases does this pathway explain?
Our solutions:** 65-75% coverage (3 valid outcomes)
High = pathways capture most routes to success
🔢 Frequency Threshold
Question:** Minimum cases to consider a pathway empirically grounded?
Our thresholds:** 2 (rare outcomes) or 3 (common outcomes)
Balances pattern detection with empirical support

Key thresholds: Necessity ≥0.90 | Sufficiency (incl.cut) ≥0.85 | n.cut = 2-3 depending on outcome prevalence

The Metrics Explained

Consistency: “How reliable is this pattern?”

\[\text{Consistency} = \frac{\text{Cases with condition AND outcome}}{\text{Cases with condition}}\]

  • 0.85+ = Reliable (85%+ of cases with this pattern succeed)
  • Below 0.85 = Too many exceptions to trust

Coverage: “How much does this explain?”

\[\text{Coverage} = \frac{\text{Cases with condition AND outcome}}{\text{All successful cases}}\]

  • High coverage = This pattern explains many successes
  • Low coverage = This is just one of many routes to success

The 5 steps we report for each outcome:

Step What it does What to look for

Why Does Prevalence Matter?

Part 6: What Should FCDO Do?

Recommendation 1: Mandate the Prerequisites

For ALL programmes, require:

✅ Impact Strategy

Document clear, evidence-based impact pathways

100% of exceptional programmes had this

✅ M&E Systems

Strengthen monitoring and evaluation over time

98% of exceptional programmes did this

✅ Strategic Clarity

Articulate contribution to broader goals

96% of exceptional programmes had this

Note

These won’t guarantee excellence, but you almost certainly won’t achieve it without them

Recommendation 2: Design for Your Goal

For Impact

Prioritize accessibility

The simplest path: ensure outputs are affordable and reachable

Or: build equitable partnerships

For Sustainability

Choose your route

Either: - Invest in quality + capacity, OR - Embed institutionally + extend timeline

Recommendation 3: Make Equity Intentional

GESI focus is non-negotiable for equity outcomes

The evidence is clear:

  • GESI appears in 100% of equity recipes
  • 91% of equity-achieving programmes had GESI focus (necessity)
  • GESI coverage is 93% — it explains most equity success

. . .

This means:

Equity cannot be an afterthought or a “nice to have.” It must be integrated from design through implementation.

Caveats & Limitations

📝
Rhetorical Adaptation?
90% claim crisis response. Are they actually adapting, or just saying they adapted?
🔄
System Circularity
FCDO rewards ToC & MEL. Programmes with these score highly. Finding or tautology?
🎲
Unmeasured Factors
Leadership, luck, political windows — we can't capture these in the data.
📊
Self-Reported Data
Business Cases & ARs are self-reported. Incentives to present favourably.

The Big Picture

Key Takeaway

Different outcomes require different approaches

There is no single “best practice” configuration that works for everything.

If you want… Focus on…
Exceptional ratings Prerequisites (necessary but not sufficient)
Impact Output accessibility (simplest path)
Sustainability Quality delivery OR institutional embedding
Equity Explicit GESI integration (mandatory)

Key Messages for Policy Makers

📦
"Accessibility is the gateway to impact"
OutputsAccessible alone is sufficient for impact. Prioritize open access, plain-language outputs, stakeholder-friendly formats.
♀️
"GESI is non-negotiable for equity"
Gender & Social Inclusion appears in 100% of equity pathways. Embed from design — not an afterthought.
🔀
"Pick your sustainability strategy"
Three paths: (1) Accessible + Capacity, (2) Governance + Embedding + Long-term funding, or (3) Long-term funding without competitive selection. FundingLongevity appears in 2/3 pathways.
⚖️
"You can't optimize for everything"
HIGH_PERFORMANCE fails completely. Be explicit about which outcomes matter most. Stop pretending every programme can maximize all.

Practical Implications

For Impact
• Mandate open access** for all research outputs
• Require plain-language summaries**
• Design stakeholder-friendly formats** from the start
This is the simplest, most powerful lever.
For Equity
• GESI is a design requirement**, not a reporting line
• Build GESI into ToC, not just logframes
• Accept that some "efficient" impact paths are off-limits
This settles the "nice to have" debate.
For Sustainability
• Three routes:** outputs, ownership, or long-term funding
• If outputs: invest in both accessible AND capacity
• If ownership: governance + local embedding + multi-year
FundingLongevity appears in 2 of 3 pathways.
For Programme Design
• Be explicit about priority outcomes**
• Don't assume all outcomes can be maximized
• Potential trade-offs exist (e.g., ~GESI in one impact path)
Honest priority-setting beats false promises.
